# Evidence: 8 to 12 short conversations (T5 asks for real ones)

Talk to people who live with the problem, and to people who handle complaints. 10 minutes each, in person or on a call. Write down the date, who they are (role, not necessarily name), and what they said in their own words.

## Residents, shopkeepers, hostel or PG residents, a school administrator
1. The last time water stopped or a road broke near you: what happened, and what did you do?
2. Who did you tell? How (phone, app, in person)? What happened next? How long until it was fixed?
3. Did you ever stop complaining because nothing happened?
4. If you could see "9 neighbours confirmed this" and the status of your complaint, would you use it? What would stop you?
5. Would you speak your complaint in Hindi instead of typing? Would you share your phone number to verify?
6. Which other problem hurts more than potholes: waterlogging, garbage, streetlights? (This decides the second category.)

## Complaint handlers: ward or municipal office clerk, junior engineer, resident-association secretary
1. How do complaints reach you today? How many a day or week?
2. How do you decide which to act on first? How do you know a complaint is real or a duplicate?
3. Have you seen fake, repeated or abusive complaints? What did you do?
4. What happens when a complaint is not fixed, or the citizen says it is not fixed? Who do you report to?
5. What would you need to trust a tool like this? Who would pay for it, and who decides?

## After each conversation
One line: what surprised you, and what you would change in the product. After 5 conversations, write the named user in three sentences (T5 question 1).
